The series

Every episode, the Wndr(er) stops, notices something ordinary, and turns it into a story.

A puddle becomes an ocean. A shadow becomes a giant. A crack in the pavement becomes a road to somewhere else.

WndrKlub shows children that anything becomes extraordinary the moment you put a frame around it. And then it hands them the pencil.

Meet the Wndr(er)

The Wndr(er) is a creative explorer, and the star of the show.

They notice what everyone else walks past: the sway of a tree, the reflection in a window, the sound a door makes when it closes slowly. They pause where others move on, framing the ordinary until it becomes extraordinary.

A filmmaker before they know the word.

How an episode works

Five panels, every time.

Short enough that watching isn’t the activity. Long enough to open a door.

The look

Whatever comes to hand.

Hand-drawn line, live action, photographs, cut paper, the textures of a child’s art table. A drawn Wndr(er) walks through a real street. A real puddle sits inside a sketched panel.

If a child watches and thinks “I could make that”, it’s working.

The grammar

Panels become shots.
Page turns become edits.
Flipbooks become animation.

a flipbook, alive ↑
A panel is a rectangle drawn around a moment. It says: look at this one thing, now.
A gutter is the gap between panels. Where the viewer does the work, filling the blank, making the leap.
A flipbook is animation at its most basic. Draw, flick through, the dot walks. The whole medium in a gesture.

Built from the oldest animation tools there are, so children can see how it’s made and do it themselves.

Beyond the screen

WndrKlub keeps going after the episode ends.

Designed alongside Scotland’s Film and Screen Education curriculum, it gives teachers a five-minute episode, a folded piece of paper and a pencil. That’s the whole kit.

Young animators trained in Scotland run workshops where children make films of their own.
